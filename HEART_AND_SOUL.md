# HEART_AND_SOUL.md
## Design doctrine for the Synthia™ studio

> Perfection is reached when there is nothing left to remove without harming the user's ability to succeed.

Good design is the removal of friction, not the accumulation of features.
Build the minimum necessary interface around the maximum useful capability.

## The designer's question

Not "what else can we add?" but "what is this person trying to do, and what can
we remove between them and that outcome?"

The goal: minimum cognitive effort, minimum physical effort, maximum confidence,
maximum usefulness.

## Start with intent

Every product, page and video has one primary job, stated in one sentence.
If you cannot state it, you do not understand the work yet. Identify:

1. What the person came to do.
2. Why they need it now.
3. What would make it feel effortless.
4. What could confuse, delay or overwhelm them.
5. What must exist for them to trust the result.

## The essential path

**Enter → Act → Review → Complete → Recover/Reuse.** Every screen, scene and
interaction earns its place on this path. Remove anything that duplicates an
action, exists only because the technology allows it, needs explaining, interrupts
the main task, exposes implementation, or asks a decision the system can safely make.

## Complexity belongs behind the interface

Prefer **system detects → system prepares → person confirms** over
**person configures → person manages → person fixes**.
Automation removes work. It never removes the human check on consequential,
shared, paid, published or irreversible actions.

## Every element earns its place

For every button, card, sentence, animation, metric, setting or scene, ask:
does this help the person understand, decide, act, verify, recover or succeed?
If not, remove it. Attention is finite.

## Delight is effortlessness

The system understood. The next step was obvious. Nothing unnecessary appeared.
Mistakes were easy to undo. It was faster than expected. Decoration is not delight.

## The subtraction test

Can it be removed? Remove it. Combined? Combine it. Inferred? Automate it.
Shown only when needed? Hide it until then. Understood without explanation? If not,
simplify. Fewer decisions possible? Reduce them. Belongs elsewhere? Move it.
Stop when the next removal would hurt usefulness, confidence, accessibility or control.

## Decision order

Optimize in this order. Never trade a higher item for a lower one.

1. Outcome for the person
2. Clarity
3. Trust and truthfulness
4. Ease and cognitive load
5. Accessibility
6. Reliability and security
7. Speed and performance
8. Conversion or measurable result
9. Distinction and visual craft
10. Novelty

*(v4.0 change: this unifies the two orders that existed. The earlier
HEART_AND-SOUL ranked speed above accessibility and had no slot for reliability or
conversion. The Website Governor order is adopted. See `_factory/canon/SOURCES.md`.)*

## Non-negotiable rules

- Do not expose system complexity because it exists.
- Do not add functionality without naming the pain it solves.
- Do not make people do work the system can safely do.
- Do not automate consequential decisions without review.
- Do not let secondary features compete with the primary job.
- Do not mistake visual minimalism for functional simplicity.
- Do not hide information needed for trust.
- Do not keep a feature because it was already built.
- Do not invent proof: no fake metrics, logos, reviews or results.

## Studio rules (added in v4.0)

- **Proof sells, not adjectives.** Every delivered job leaves a before/after case
  study, with the client's permission, in `08_learn`. That portfolio is the sales engine.
- **Build systems, not single assets.** A request for one video is a chance to make
  a template that renders the next ten.
- **Surprising after understandable, never instead of understandable.**

## Final standard

> What can I remove while making the person more successful?

Make the system powerful. Make the experience simple. Keep the human in control.
Remove everything else.
